1. The model

Shows: customers can reach a person whenever they want, and Wiz hands over what it cannot solve.

When a customer opens BrightMove's support chat, Wiz, BrightMove's AI support agent, answers first, at any hour. Wiz answers using LightHub, BrightMove's online help center of how-to articles. When Wiz cannot solve a question, a person on the support team takes over, and engineering fixes software bugs and problems connecting BrightMove with other tools. See the Overview page for support hours and response targets.

Routing the first message. When a customer starts a chat, an automated rule reads the first message and sends it down one of three paths:

  1. The customer asks for a person. If the message includes phrases like "talk to a person," "agent," "human" or "live agent," Wiz steps aside. The chat is tagged, the customer is told a person will respond within two hours, and the chat goes straight to the support team. This path has been used in more than 550 customer conversations since January 2026.
  2. The customer reports a possible outage. If the message mentions an outage, glitch, bug or the system being down, a live system status check runs. If there is a known issue, the customer is pointed to BrightMove's status page, status.brightmove.com. If there is not, or the check itself fails, the chat is assigned to the support team for a person to investigate. A reported outage is never left with only the AI.
  3. Everything else. Wiz answers from LightHub articles and the written instructions the team gives it, called its guidance. Wiz’s guidance is to offer a person after two attempts that don’t resolve the question. When the chat is handed over, a person picks it up with the whole conversation in front of them and stays responsible for the outcome. Wiz also reminds customers they can ask for a person at any time.

Example: one line gets a person. In October 2026, a customer's first message was "talk to a person." The chat was routed within seconds, a support team member replied about 2 minutes later, and the login problem was fixed with engineering that morning.

Example: two attempts, then a person. On October 29, 2026, a customer could not save a record. Wiz gave two answers and reminded the customer they could ask for a person. The customer did, and a support team member replied about 3 minutes later. They found a required custom field blocking the save and fixed it in the same chat. The customer rated the conversation 5 out of 5.

Example: two attempts, then an audit. On October 30, 2026, a customer asked why jobs were closing before their end date. Wiz gave two answers, first the end date setting, then workflow triggers. Neither explained it, so the customer asked for a person and was transferred within seconds. A support team member replied under 3 minutes later and had a developer check the job’s history. It had closed on its original end date; the date was later extended but the job was never reopened, and the team explained how to reopen it.

2. People audit the AI

Shows: the weekly review applies to Wiz conversations too.

Each week BrightMove's support team reviews Wiz conversations, including every negative experience score and satisfaction rating. Findings become changes to Wiz's guidance or to LightHub articles.

Sample of Wiz’s guidance. These are excerpts from Wiz’s live guidance in Intercom, read on October 30, 2026. The first rule is the source of the two-attempts rule in Section 1.

No More Merry-Go-Round — Escalate When Repeating (updated April 23, 2026)

“If you have already given the same answer (or a variation of it) twice in a conversation and the user is still pushing back, asking the same question differently, or expressing frustration — do not repeat it a third time. Instead, acknowledge the loop and offer to connect them with a human.”

Ask for a person, get a person

“If the customer asks to speak to a human or indicates they do not want AI help, escalate immediately. Treat phrases like agent, human, person, representative, support agent, live agent, live chat, supervisor, real person, talk to someone, or connect me to support as direct handoff requests. Do not ask clarifying questions. Do not offer to continue with Fin. Reply once with: ‘Transferring you to a human agent now.’ Then stop and hand the conversation off.”

Escalate for Feedback (updated August 5, 2026)

“When a user gives any feedback about BrightMove, immediately escalate it to a human teammate for review rather than attempting to resolve it yourself. Acknowledge that you’ve received the feedback and greatly appreciate it. Let the user know it’s being passed along, without promising a specific fix, outcome, or timeline.”

Source: Intercom, Fin AI Agent guidance. Wiz appears in Intercom as “Fin”.

Example: from a missed SMS answer to new guidance

  1. April 20, 2026: the miss. A customer asked how to send an email and text blast to candidates. Wiz explained how to send SMS (text messages), but the customer’s account does not include Engage SMS, BrightMove’s texting add-on. The customer gave the conversation the lowest rating.
  2. April 27, 2026: the review. A support team member reviewed the conversation and noted that Wiz should account for whether a customer has the add-on, or ask a clarifying question first.
  3. May 18, 2026: the new guidance. The team added the SMS/Texting Escalation guidance: If a user mentions SMS or texting, let them know that Engage SMS is an add-on feature and escalate to a human immediately.
  4. Checking that it works. The same day, an internal test confirmed that Wiz now hands SMS questions to a person. A second internal test on June 26, 2026 confirmed that Wiz also explains that Engage SMS is an add-on. Both after examples are internal tests, not customer conversations.

The customer experience score is about 92% whether Wiz handles the chat alone (92.4%) or together with a person (92.5%). Wiz brings the speed, and people make sure there’s quality in every response.

Wiz resolution rate (conversations Wiz resolved): 82.4%. Wiz deflection rate (conversations that did not need to go to a person): 68.7%. See Wiz measure definitions for how each rate is counted.

Monthly trend: in the “Fin’s impact over time” chart, Wiz’s experience score stays roughly between the mid-80s and high-90s every month except a dip in December 2025. Resolution rises from about 70% in October 2025 to the high 80s by August and September 2026. Deflection rises from the low 50s to the high 70s, with the clearest step up between January and February 2026.

A process change in the same period: Wiz can only answer from content BrightMove has published, so a new feature with no LightHub (help center) article is a question Wiz cannot answer. In early 2026, BrightMove stopped waiting for a customer to ask about a feature before writing its article. LightHub articles are now written and published with each release, so Wiz has an answer for new features from day one. BrightMove builds software in short development cycles; the practice started informally during one cycle and was made official on March 5, 2026. Since then, the script the team uses to demonstrate each cycle’s changes has a “KB Notes” (knowledge base notes) column that records the article work for every change.
